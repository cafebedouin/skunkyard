## Seat claims checked

- **Gemini's "1,000,000 cost units per transaction" is wrong for mainnet.** 1,000,000 is the devnet's
  `maxBlockCost`; mainnet's is 8,001,091 per block with a relay cap of 4,900,000 per transaction (`q2/RESULT.md`).
  Its kill criterion for the liveness bond is therefore far looser than stated.
- **ChatGPT's 96 KB relay cap agrees** with `notes/2026-10-02-gemini-reading.md`.
- **Grok's storage rent facts agree with ours:** a box untouched for 1,051,200 blocks can be spent without its
  script, and if its value covers the fee it is recreated with the same script, registers and tokens. Hence its
  rule that rent cannot serve as an inheritance trigger.
- **Qwen's "salvage contract" (idea 1) cannot exist.** A script guards its own box; it cannot authorize spending
  someone else's aged box. Rent collection is already open to any miner. Dropped.
- **DeepThink's rent ideas (1, 5) rest on a misreading.** Rent takes a fee, not the whole box, and registers and
  tokens survive it. Its own kill criteria fail on the first fact; dropped.
- **Qwen's [UNVERIFIED] on Merkle verification:** ErgoScript has native authenticated AVL trees (`AvlTree`), used
  in the many-time keys skunk. Its idea 6 is expressible; not new.
- **Perplexity's [UNVERIFIED] on proving absence** (insurance pays if a required transition did not happen) has
  an answer the seat missed, by reasoning, still to be confirmed on a devnet: put the stale state box in as a
  *data input*. A data input must be unspent, so the transaction proves that the box was not replaced, at a height
  the script compares with the deadline. If it was replaced, there is nothing to claim.
- **Grok 2's header facts** (`CONTEXT.preHeader.minerPk`, the last ten headers) agree with `research/inclusion/README.md`.

## What the convergence means for us

**1. The liveness bounty is the crowd's strongest idea, and it composes with ours rather than replacing it.**
DeepThink's objection to keeper execution by miners is fair: a pool client is not permissionless. But the two
designs differ in reach. A rising bounty needs the protocol written with a reserve and a stale path, which a
deployed protocol like Dexy does not have; the Lithos executor works on deployed protocols whenever the trigger
needs nothing but a fee (still [UNVERIFIED] for Dexy). New protocols want the bounty, with mining clients as the
default executor that rarely lets it rise. The attack every seat named first is the same: a cartel or miner delays
maintenance to let the bounty grow, or performs a maintenance step that does no work ("maintenance theater",
Perplexity). The second is the real one; the bounty must be bound to a checkable change of state.

**2. Keyless post-quantum migration has a hole five seats missed.** While the old `proveDlog` path stays valid, a
quantum attacker spends through it, and an inactivity trigger or a migration bounty does nothing about that. Only
a design that turns the old key off at a fixed height (Grok 8, ChatGPT 5's two eras) helps, and only for boxes
created with the successor committed, never for the boxes already exposed (96.58% of non-protocol ERG at height
1,886,343, `q1/RESULT.md`). This belongs with SK-036's question C5 and the post-quantum roadmap, as "new boxes can
schedule their own retirement of the curve", not as an agent idea.

**3. Two seat ideas improve the fence directly.** The policy singleton read by data input (Grok 5) replaces a
whitelist baked into every fenced box; the capability view (Perplexity 3) says the fenced key may as well be
public. SK-005's design should take both before A1 is costed.

**4. The case against AI resolvers is an argument from priors; the experiment answers it with data.** The seats
attacked "AI decides what is true". The proposal on the table is narrower: named sources, mechanical rules written
before the event (as forecasting tournaments write them), and resolution anyone can rerun. Their objections that
survive that narrowing are three: proving what was fetched, sources revised after the fact, and bribes larger than
the bonds. The disagreement-rate experiment (historical questions, archived sources, several models, measured
against official resolutions, per class of source) measures how often a challenge would fire, which is the number
all five critiques assume without measuring. Perplexity's evidence boxes (idea 8) are a useful fallback shape if
the rates are poor: the chain checks that a decision consumed a stated quantity and diversity of bonded evidence,
not that the evidence is true.

## Ranked moves

1. **Prior art first** (`ROADMAP.md`, R0). Most clusters above have relatives elsewhere: keeper networks, intent
   solvers, optimistic oracles, streams, vault covenants, inheritance wallets, dispute games.
2. **Liveness bounty skunk**, with the data-input proof of absence as its second question.
3. **AI resolver agreement experiment** (no chain needed).
4. **Fence redesign** with a policy singleton and the capability view, then SK-005's A1 to A5 as preregistered.
5. **Scheduled retirement of the curve** for new boxes, filed under the post-quantum line.
